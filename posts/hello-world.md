Every coding journey starts with a "Hello World", so it felt right to start this blog the same way! I'm Marco Araujo, a Fullstack Software Engineer from Brazil.

## Where did I start?

My first steps came back in 2014, when I took a **technical course** in São Paulo's ETEC as a **Programming Assistant**. There, I learned the basics of system analysis, SQL databases, web development with JavaScript and PHP, and desktop software with Java and C#.
ETEC is one of the leading technical education schools in Brazil, so although each topic was explored in 6-month modules, the coursework was pretty demanding, requiring a lot of effort, patience, overnight debugging, and finding out semi-colons are the root of all evil.
I learned and grew a lot during that period, and that sealed my decision to join Engineering.

In 2016, I started my **bachelor's degree in Computer Engineering** at Rio de Janeiro State University (UERJ). The STEM foundation was really strong, with the basic engineering cycle featuring several calculus and physics courses, differential equations, numerical simulation for physical phenomena, linear algebra, and much more! On the computer science and engineering cycle, the course also provided a rich set of subjects, like algorithms, data structures, computer architecture, databases, networks, and state machines, and I also had the chance to explore machine learning concepts like regression models, perceptrons, neural networks, and applied linear algebra techniques, like SVD, QR decomposition, PCA, and more.
The constant exchange with students and Ph.D. researchers provided an even richer experience, and I got to attend meetups and seminars, ranging from sociophysics to black holes.

## Learning by building

In 2017, I joined **Serra Jr. Engenharia**, our university junior company, as a **Junior Project Manager & Engineer**. Under the mentorship of experienced staff and Ph.D. professors, I managed backlogs, gathered client requirements, and led the delivery of web applications using Angular, PHP, and JavaScript. It was also where I designed an industrial IoT solution with Arduino and C++ to automate temperature control in production, where I first put my love for electronics to use!

In 2019, I joined a lab in the university as an **IT Intern**, where I developed a climate-monitoring mobile app with Ionic and a Java REST API. The goal was to help the community and authorities with disaster prevention, through alerts based on strategic placement of sensors for measuring precipitation and water levels, among other atmospheric variables. The app was published on the Google Play Store and earned a project registration certificate, and became one of the projects I'm proudest of.

## From college to the job market

In 2020, I took on a **freelance** project with Satélite Norte, building an audit platform. This was the first project I ran by myself, reaching out to a client, having meetings to collect all requirements, prototyping the solution, designing the UI/UX, planning tasks, and implementing everything. I built three different frontends using React and React Native, along with a Node.js and PostgreSQL REST API. The experience was pretty fun, and the client feedback was great!
The system is now being used to audit hundreds of agencies, and can provide centralized insights, planning, and requests.

## EasyRetro: serverless apps

Later in 2020, I joined **EasyRetro**, where I spent nearly five years working fully remote and async. I handled end-to-end product development across multiple SaaS applications, including EasyRetro, Slideasy, and PlanITPoker, from feature planning all the way to release. Being a small startup, I've got to wear multiple hats, working from discovery and prototyping to implementation, analytics, SEO, and even helping with customer support for a few weeks. That diversity of roles made me mature very quickly.

Besides working on EasyRetro, which was the main focus, I built Slideasy from scratch up to its MVP launch, and led the migration of PlanITPoker to a new stack while it moved from free to paid.

The **serverless** stack also won my heart! Backend services like Firebase provide top-notch authentication, databases, hosting, file storage, and cloud functions that are really easy to set up and ship at virtually no cost, since the free tier can accommodate small to medium size apps, allowing one to validate, pivot, and grow before spending a dime on infrastructure.
Of course this comes with vendor lock-in, but being based on platforms like Google, I would say it's a pretty safe bet!

## Form Factory: performance at scale

In 2025, I joined **Form Factory**, a California-based company, where I worked on **headless e-commerce** for leading US brands. Working at this scale has had a huge impact on my mindset and how I approach building!

I led a performance optimization program that took Lighthouse and Core Web Vitals scores from poor to production standards, and rebuilt the Customer Hub for a major clothing brand, integrating a new loyalty provider and covering account management, orders, and customer support. I was also tasked with refactoring the Product pages, enhancing UX, optimizing the components tree and rendering performance while applying modern styling.

The seasonal demand in businesses such as e-commerce was new to me, and it was pretty interesting to have windows for development, shipping, and validation, followed by cleanup, hardening, and discovery work. It seemed pretty much like leaning back to sprint forward a few times each year!

I worked in something like a Forward Deployed role, reporting to the merchant staff in the US, so translating technical constraints, proposing solutions and presenting proof-of-concepts was a weekly activity that I really enjoyed!

## What you'll find here

Today, my main tools are **TypeScript**, **React**, **Node.js**, **Remix** / **React Router**, **Shopify Hydrogen**, **Vue**, **Firebase**, and **PostgreSQL**. Lately, I've also been diving into **AI tooling**, working with **Claude Code**, the **Claude API**, **MCP**, and **agent skills**.

On this blog, I plan to write about the things I work with and care about: JavaScript and TypeScript, web performance, SaaS and e-commerce, AI, etc. Mostly practical lessons learned along the way.
